I run a YouTube channel called [People Watching](https://www.youtube.com/@PeopleWatchingMarcus) — 4K walking videos through cities. No talking, just streets. It makes $250-350/mo profit, which basically funds the cameras.

85% of my views are from search. That's great for discovery. It's terrible for community.

People watch, then leave. No comment. No subscribe. The algorithm sees a one-and-done viewer and moves on.

I tried all the classic CTAs:

*   "Like and subscribe"
*   "Comment where you're watching from"
*   "What city should I do next?"

All duds. 0.2% comment rate on a good day.

### The insight

Walking videos are taste tests.

When you watch a walk through Beverly Hills at golden hour, or a rainy walk in Copenhagen at 7am, you instantly have an opinion. You don't think about it, you *feel* it. "That street is gorgeous." "That neighborhood is overrated." "I would never live there."

Search viewers already have the taste. They just don't have permission to fight about it.

### Idea #4: The Taste Fight

I started testing one line, about 45 seconds into the walk, on-screen text only:

> **Overrated or underrated? — fight in the comments.**

That's it. No voiceover. Just text over the walk.

Sometimes I make it specific to the location:

> "Rodeo Drive at 5pm on a Saturday — overrated or actually worth the hype?"
> "Copenhagen bike traffic — chaotic or beautiful?"
> "This beach walk — would you pay $30 for a sunbed here?"

The pattern is always the same: `[specific taste judgment] + [invitation to disagree]`.

### Why it works

1.  **It's low effort.** You don't need to know my channel. You just need eyes. Anyone can have a take in 2 seconds.

2.  **It's identity.** People *love* defending their taste. "Are you crazy? Cala Bassa is the best cove in Ibiza" gets 12 replies. That's retention.

3.  **It happens early.** 45 seconds is before the big drop-off in my retention graph. If they comment, they stay to see if someone argues back.

4.  **It doesn't break the vibe.** My walks are silent. A voice yelling "SMASH THAT LIKE BUTTON" ruins it. Text on screen keeps the walk intact.

### The numbers

Before: 0.2% comment rate, 31% avg viewed
After 5 videos with the CTA: 1.1% comment rate, 38% avg viewed

Comments went from "nice video" to actual threads:

> "No way Rodeo is overrated, you just went on the wrong day"
> "Underrated. People sleep on this side of BH."
> "Overrated and you know it, Marcus"

That last one is my favorite. They know me now.

### How I do it technically

DaVinci Resolve, one text+ node:

- Fade in at 0:42, hold 4 seconds, fade out
- Font: Inter Bold, 85% white, slightly off-center so it doesn't cover faces
- Exported as part of the master, not a YouTube end screen — so it survives embeds

I also add a pinned comment with my own take to seed it. "My take: overrated before 10am, perfect after. Change my mind." That comment gets more likes than the video half the time.

### If you make walking / POV / no-talk videos

Don't ask people to subscribe. Ask them to judge.

Search viewers came for the location. They'll stay for the argument about it.

My next test is making the title itself the fight: "Is This the Most Beautiful Street in LA?" vs "Walking Beverly Hills." Same walk, different framing. I'll report back.
