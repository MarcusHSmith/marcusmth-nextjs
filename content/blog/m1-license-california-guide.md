I want a Yamaha Zuma 125. It's a 125cc scooter, which means in California I need an M1 motorcycle license to ride it legally. No M1, no Zuma. So I'm getting the license, and I set myself a ceiling of $425 all-in.

Here's the whole process as I understand it, and the path I'm taking. I'm in the middle of this now — I'll update the post when the license is in hand.

## Why the M1 matters for a 125cc scooter

California has two motorcycle license classes. M2 covers mopeds and motorized bicycles. M1 covers everything with two wheels, including a 125cc scooter like the Zuma. There is no "it's just a scooter" exception at 125cc — that's M1 territory.

The practical upside for me: motorcycle parking at the Playa Vista campus is free, and a Zuma sips gas on the Beverly Hills ↔ Playa Vista run. The license is the gate.

## The two paths

If you're over 21 (I am), California gives you two ways to get the M1:

**Path 1: DMV start to finish.** Study the California Motorcycle Handbook, pass the knowledge test at the DMV, get an instruction permit, practice, then pass the DMV skills test on your own bike. Cheapest path — you're mostly paying DMV fees — but the skills test is on a real course with tight low-speed maneuvers, and you need a bike to practice on and to test with.

**Path 2: CMSP Basic RiderCourse.** Take the California Motorcyclist Safety Program's ~2-day Basic RiderCourse. They provide the training bikes, which solves the biggest chicken-and-egg problem: you can't practice without a bike, and you shouldn't buy the bike before you know you want to ride. Graduates get a DL 389 certificate that waives the DMV riding skills test. You still take the written knowledge test and vision test at the DMV.

I'm taking Path 2. The course costs around $350 depending on the training site, which fits under my $425 ceiling, and training on their bikes before I spend a dollar on a Zuma is the right order of operations.

## The permit restrictions (worth knowing)

While you're on an instruction permit: no passengers, no riding at night, and no freeways. Fine for parking-lot practice and surface streets, which is all a new rider should be doing anyway.

## My study setup

I'm drilling the handbook with flashcards — road signs, right-of-way, lane positioning, the stuff the written test actually asks. The written test is the easy part if you don't wing it; most failures come from people who skimmed the handbook once.

## Total cost math

- CMSP Basic RiderCourse: ~$350
- DMV application / license fees: the remainder
- Ceiling: $425 all-in

If the course comes in under $350 somewhere nearby, great. I'm not price-optimizing this to death — the expensive mistake would be buying the scooter first and discovering I hate riding.

## The plan

1. Finish the written-test prep
2. Book the CMSP Basic RiderCourse
3. Pass the DMV knowledge + vision test with the DL 389 in hand
4. Walk out with the M1, then go scooter shopping
