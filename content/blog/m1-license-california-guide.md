I want a Yamaha Zuma 125. It's a 125cc scooter, which means in California I need an M1 motorcycle license to ride it legally. No M1, no Zuma. So I'm getting the license, and I set myself a ceiling of $425 all-in.

Here's the whole process as I understand it, and the path I'm taking. I'm in the middle of this now — I'll update the post when the license is in hand.

## Why the M1 matters for a 125cc scooter

California has two motorcycle license classes. Class M2 covers motorized bicycles, mopeds, or any bicycle with an attached motor. Class M1 covers any two-wheeled motorcycle — and everything under M2. A 125cc scooter like the Zuma is a motor-driven cycle (anything under 150cc), and the DMV is explicit: motor-driven cycles require an M1. There is no "it's just a scooter" exception at 125cc — that's M1 territory.

The practical upside for me: motorcycle parking at the Playa Vista campus is free, and a Zuma sips gas on the Beverly Hills ↔ Playa Vista run. The license is the gate.

## The two paths

If you're over 21 (I am), California gives you two ways to get the M1:

**Path 1: DMV start to finish.** Study the California Motorcycle Handbook, pass the knowledge test at the DMV, get an instruction permit, practice, then pass the DMV skills test on your own bike. Cheapest path — you're mostly paying DMV fees — but the skills test is on a real course with tight low-speed maneuvers, and you need a bike to practice on and to test with. One nuance I learned: if you're 21 or older, you can actually go straight to the driving test without holding a permit first. The permit is still the legal way to practice on public streets beforehand.

**Path 2: CMSP Motorcyclist Training Course (MTC).** The California Motorcyclist Safety Program's course for new riders: 15 hours total — 5 in the classroom, 10 on the bike — usually run over a weekend (some sites do two days, some three). They provide the training bikes, which solves the biggest chicken-and-egg problem: you can't practice without a bike, and you shouldn't buy the bike before you know you want to ride. Graduates get a DL 389 certificate that waives the DMV riding skills test (use it within 12 months of the issue date). You still take the written knowledge test and vision test at the DMV.

I'm taking Path 2. One wrinkle: the ~$350 price I'd seen floating around is the old state-set tuition. Every current training site I checked lists $425 for riders 21 and over — which is my entire $425 ceiling before DMV fees even stack on top. So either my ceiling moves, or I commit to Path 1. Training on their bikes before I spend a dollar on a Zuma is still the right order of operations, so I'm leaning toward letting the ceiling move.

## The permit restrictions (worth knowing)

While you're on an instruction permit: no passengers, no riding at night, and no freeways. Fine for parking-lot practice and surface streets, which is all a new rider should be doing anyway.

## My study setup

I'm drilling the handbook with flashcards — road signs, right-of-way, lane positioning, the stuff the written test actually asks. The written test is the easy part if you don't wing it; most failures come from people who skimmed the handbook once.

## Total cost math

- CMSP Motorcyclist Training Course: $425 (current tuition at the sites I checked for 21+; the ~$350 I'd written earlier is outdated)
- DMV application / license fees: stack on top
- Ceiling: $425 all-in — the course alone now eats the whole budget, so something in this math has to give

If I find a site running the course cheaper, great. I'm not price-optimizing this to death — the expensive mistake would be buying the scooter first and discovering I hate riding.

## The plan

1. Finish the written-test prep
2. Book the CMSP Motorcyclist Training Course
3. Pass the DMV knowledge + vision test with the DL 389 in hand
4. Walk out with the M1, then go scooter shopping
