I'm running the 2027 LA Marathon — 26.2 miles from Dodger Stadium to Century City on March 7, 2027. Registration is done. This post is my starting line: where my fitness actually is right now, and the plan for the next six months.

No race recap, no finish-line wisdom. Just the base build, documented as it happens.

## Where I'm starting

- **Running Fitness: 84** (COROS) — the aerobic base is there, barely
- **~10 miles per week**, all easy — coming back from a foot layoff
- **Daily lifting + running** — the routine is solid, the volume isn't

Six months out, the job isn't speed. It's rebuilding the aerobic base without re-injuring the foot, then stacking volume patiently until the real marathon block starts.

## How I'm tracking everything

Every run gets logged across five sources, and I reconcile them in one pass:

1. **Apple Health** — daily steps, resting HR, HRV, sleep
2. **COROS Training Hub** — Running Fitness, training load, session data
3. **WHOOP** — recovery, strain, sleep debt
4. **Strava** — the social record and segment history
5. **My workout log spreadsheet** — the source of truth I actually read

When sources disagree, the strap wins. Wrist optical heart rate lies during intervals; the chest strap doesn't.

## The base-build rules

**All easy, until it isn't.** Every run is conversational pace. The fastest way to ruin a marathon build six months out is to race your training partners in October.

**Volume before intensity.** The progression is weekly mileage, not pace. Something like 10 → 15 → 20 → 25+ over the base phase, with a down week every fourth week. Intensity gets introduced only once the volume base can absorb it.

**Zone 2 is the job.** Most base miles sit in Zone 2 — the pace where you can hold a conversation but wouldn't want to. Conservative and boring beats aggressive and injured.

**Strength stays.** Lifting isn't cross-training decoration; it's the injury-prevention program. The foot layoff taught me that the hard way.

**Sleep is training.** WHOOP recovery scores drive the day's call — green means run the plan, yellow means easy only, red means rest. Arguing with the recovery score is how you get a second layoff.

## What I'm watching

- **Resting HR and HRV trends** — the earliest signal that volume is outpacing recovery
- **Running Fitness (COROS)** — should climb slowly through the base phase
- **Foot feedback** — any sharp pain, not soreness, and the run ends immediately
- **Weight trend** — marathon training at higher mileage quietly undereats; the scale catches it before performance does

## The honest part

I've never run a marathon. Everything above is the plan, not the result. The value of writing it down now is that in March I can look back and see exactly where the plan survived contact with reality and where it didn't.
